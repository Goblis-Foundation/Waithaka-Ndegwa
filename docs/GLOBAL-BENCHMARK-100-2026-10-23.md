# Waithaka Ndegwa: global website benchmark and recommendations
**23 October 2026 · Africa/Nairobi**

## Decision brief

Keep the website a personal publishing and practice platform with a clear African starting point. Its distinctive proposition is the connection between **original thinking, visible venture-building and evidence of results**. A larger menu, more decorative cards or more AI-generated articles will not establish that connection.

The immediate homepage correction is implemented: the region labels now read **Kenya → East Africa → Africa → Europe → United Kingdom → United States → Canada → Elsewhere**. The accompanying paragraph refers to “East Africa and the rest of Africa.” This makes the continental scope explicit without changing the local starting point.

The next design priority should be to make three questions easy to answer: **What is Waithaka working on? What can I learn here? How can I engage appropriately?**

This report contains recommendations, not a claim that a complete second redesign, every account connection or new editorial content has been published. The explicit homepage correction is the implementation in this research batch.

## What was researched—and what “100” means

This is a purposive benchmark of **100 distinct public reference destinations**, comprising **48 practitioner/author-led references and 52 adjacent organisations, publications and innovation ecosystems**. These include African practitioners, global authors, social-enterprise networks, Asian practice organisations, Australian social-innovation organisations and Latin American impact institutions. The larger organisations are useful for evidence, resources and case-study structure; their scale and administrative menus are unsuitable templates for a personal site.

There is no substantiated worldwide “top 100 similar sites” ranking behind this list. Sites were selected for relevance to the requested functions, not traffic, prestige, claimed impact or measured conversion. Numbering is a register, not a league table.

Public first-party page text, navigation, headings and links were screened. Ten additional collection/resource/measurement destinations were followed for more detailed content-architecture checks. Search-indexed first-party text was used where direct retrieval failed. Of the final 100 references:

- **90** had usable directly retrieved first-party page or collection text.
- **5** relied on indexed first-party text because the direct fetch failed or returned no content.
- **2** returned partial text containing verification prompts.
- **1** supplied only sparse homepage text.
- **2** provided evidence too limited for a current structure/design conclusion: Otto Scharmer and Honey Bee Network.

These are **not 100 visual, accessibility, mobile, security or performance audits**. No benchmark’s private analytics, conversion rate, editor, automation, CMS or commercial results were inspected. A visible feature is evidence that the feature exists, not that it improves results. Recommendations below are professional inferences that should be tested with this site’s readers.

A mistaken candidate URL for Jason Fried redirected to an unrelated business. It was replaced with his verified HEY World publication. Several inaccessible candidate pages were replaced with directly reviewed sources; unresolved limitations remain labelled. This is a reason to verify outbound identity and redirects rather than trust a plausible domain name.

The linked register at the end lists every reference, the source actually used, the evidence status and a concise observation. A machine-readable companion retains retrieval references and the same limitations.

## 3. Recommended homepage structure

The current template places the latest writing after the hero, exploration area, 2030 goals, startup journey, introductory lens and regional lens. Several strong ideas compete before the reader reaches current content. This is a source-order observation, not a measured abandonment rate.

Use this proposed sequence:

1. **Identity and promise.** Name, short positioning, one strong photograph and a principal reading action. Keep the full professional biography on About.
2. **Current work or latest thought.** One editorially selected, genuinely published item. If none exists, use a truthful current-focus introduction; do not create a fictional article.
3. **Three ways in.** Read My Thoughts; follow Startup Journeys; collaborate or invite a conversation. Resources can be a contextual link within the reading/practice paths.
4. **2030 commitments.** Three organisation groups, targets clearly labelled, current data status and a route to method/source.
5. **Selected thinking.** Up to three substantive pieces with descriptive summaries. Avoid three empty or near-identical cards.
6. **African starting point.** Keep Kenya, East Africa and Africa explicit; connect this lens to original field writing when available.
7. **A practical resource.** Feature one useful tool only when the download actually exists and its purpose is clear.
8. **Subscription and concise footer.** State what the reader will receive, link preferences and retain all six social profiles.

Move extended philosophy, repeated process statements and the longer origin story to About, with a short contextual link from the homepage. Preserve the photography and personal character; do not turn the site into a generic institutional brochure.

**Release rule:** adapt the presentation to content availability. An honest preparation state is better than a large grid of “0 thoughts” and empty destinations. Hide optional empty modules rather than fabricating activity.

## 4. Navigation, links and reading paths

Keep the current main navigation: **Home, My Thoughts, Start Here, About, Contact**, plus compact search and existing support access. The five topics remain outside the main menu as already instructed.

Recommended reading paths:

| Visitor | First useful destination | Next useful step |
| --- | --- | --- |
| New reader | Start Here: three selected pieces and a short introduction | Choose a topic or subscribe |
| Entrepreneur | Active journey or a practical resource | Read the experiment, use the tool, follow the next update |
| Potential collaborator | Relevant project summary and your actual role | Submit a purpose-labelled enquiry |
| Journalist/event organiser | Short biography, topics, approved portraits and contact | Make a speaking/media enquiry |
| Researcher/investor | Definitions, evidence and limitations behind a claim | Inspect the source or discuss a specific opportunity |
| Returning reader | Latest published update or current-focus block | Continue the relevant series |

For links:

- Give each card one clear primary destination. A title should describe the destination; “Learn more” alone is weak when repeated across a page.
- Prefer a heading link plus separately labelled topic links when cards have multiple destinations. Do not nest anchors or make a second link impossible to reach.
- When the entire surface is clickable, implement and test one coherent link pattern; do not stack several identical keyboard stops for the image, title and button.
- Keep ordinary internal reading in the same tab. Retain the requested safe new-tab behaviour for organisation logos, without adding visible “opens a new website” wording.
- Link organisation names to their own sites, a specific case to its detail page, and a progress figure to its method. Do not send every organisation card to a generic homepage if the reader needs a particular explanation.
- Review redirects and broken links on a defined maintenance cycle. Do not silently substitute a different organisation because a domain has changed.
- Add contextual next steps at article endings: one related thought, the applicable resource or the next episode, followed by an optional subscription. Avoid six competing actions.

## 5. A small card system

Use a shared visual family with different information for different tasks. Not every content block needs a bordered box or photograph.

| Card type | Essential content | Primary action | Guardrail |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Thought | Topic, clear title, specific summary, publication date, reading time | Read the thought | Use a relevant owned image, or a clean text card |
| Journey | Venture/question, phase, latest episode, last actual update | Start from the beginning or continue | Phase is not a success claim |
| Founder | Founder/venture, location, problem, stage and editorial provenance | Read the story | Publication permission and claim basis required |
| Resource | Intended user, problem, outcome, format and estimated effort | Open/use/download | Label PDF/document size and version; verify the file exists |
| Impact | Target, actual verified value or unavailable state, period, source and definition | View method/source | No animation from zero when data is missing |
| Project | Problem, your role, dates/status, partners where verified and a concrete output | Explore the work | State contribution; do not imply sole attribution |
| Event/video | Title, actual event date, duration, format and transcript availability | Watch or open the event resource | Separate past recordings from future events |

Suggested design constraints, to be validated rather than treated as universal laws: roughly 6–12 words for most card titles, 20–40 words for summaries, consistent image ratios within a group, aligned metadata, visible focus, restrained animation and adequate space between links. Allow titles to wrap naturally; do not truncate away meaning.

Use text-only lists for dense archives and long research collections. Cards are useful for selection; they are not automatically better for every information task. W3C’s design system recognises both simple cards and block-link cards and emphasises correct heading hierarchy.

## 6. Content strategy and editorial models

The differentiator should be **documented practice** rather than inspirational volume.

### My Thoughts

A durable thought should explain the question, its African/local context, the argument, the evidence, the practical implication and the limits. A short “what changed in my view” note can be more valuable than an artificial update date.

Use the existing five subjects as lenses. Connect them across articles: roads affect farm margins; market access affects venture survival; responsible technology depends on institutions. Avoid five isolated content silos.

### Startup Journeys

Continue the original one-active-venture model. Each chapter should contain a question or hypothesis, the test, what happened, evidence, cost/time where appropriate, the decision and the next step. Record failed tests and changes of direction honestly.

A proposed first sequence, once a real venture is selected:

1. The problem, baseline and assumptions.
2. What actual customer conversations changed.
3. The first test and its costs or constraints.
4. The decision to continue, revise or stop.

These are editorial outlines, not events that have already happened. Do not publish fabricated interviews, costs, customer numbers or outcomes to meet a weekly promise.

### Founder Stories

Use a repeatable profile: context, problem, approach, commercial model, evidence, constraints and lessons. Attribute founder-reported claims and distinguish them from checked records. Keep factual review, consent, corrections and relationship disclosure in the existing workflow.

### Practical resources

Prioritise four original tools: a problem-validation worksheet, an experiment log, an impact-definition sheet and a market-access readiness checklist. Each needs an HTML introduction, a genuinely usable download, an example clearly labelled illustrative and a version date. Attribute any external method and check reuse permission.

### Current focus, professional information and editorial integrity

A small dated current-focus block can show active work without creating a new main-menu page. A public professional-information page can contain approved biographies, portraits, speaking topics and media contact details. Keep it distinct from the **private dashboard Media Centre**.

Add concise editorial and AI-use explanations: what you write from experience, what requires external evidence, how corrections work and how AI assists drafting. Do not copy another author’s “no AI” stance when your chosen workflow explicitly includes reviewed AI assistance.

### Original topics worth developing

- What will qualify a startup as “successful” in the 2030 commitments?
- What changes when market access is measured as a real transaction rather than a listing?
- Five permanent jobs per enterprise: a planning assumption and a measurement responsibility.
- Why enterprise education needs a pathway into validation, finance and markets.
- What infrastructure means from the perspective of an agricultural producer.

These are proposed questions, not ready-to-publish factual articles. Research and owner input must determine their arguments.

## 7. Evidence and the 2030 commitments

Preserve the accepted targets and attribution:

- **Goblis Group:** 30 successful startups and 2,030 permanent staff.
- **Ubunifu Institute:** 2,030 incubated social enterprises; an average of five permanent staff implies a **10,150-job target inside those enterprises**, indirectly supported through incubation.
- **Goblis Foundation’s AfriTrace Link:** 30,000 African social enterprises connected to market, with a planned footprint across all **55 African countries**.

A benchmark’s “54 countries” wording is not authority to overwrite your explicit 55-country scope.

Every progress view should show the time window, source organisation, reporting date, definition and whether data is verified, self-reported, estimated or unavailable. “Successful startup”, “permanent staff”, “enterprise incubated” and “connected to market” require operational definitions. Disclose whether the same enterprise appears in more than one programme.

Indirect beneficiaries should have a separate method. Suppliers, household members, customers and communities may be relevant groups, but they cannot be converted into a numerical total by an unsupported multiplier. Track distinct people/entities where feasible, report overlap and uncertainty, and separate output, outcome and longer-term impact.

Do not create live-looking counters before source access exists. The present unavailable state is more trustworthy than an impressive invented number.

## 8. Mobile, tablet and accessibility

Recommended acceptance matrix: 320, 360 and 390 CSS-pixel phone widths; 768, 820 and 1024 tablet widths; desktop; portrait and landscape; 200% zoom; keyboard and screen-reader navigation. These are proposed test conditions, not completed results.

- Use one-column cards on narrow screens and two-column layouts where tablet space supports them. Choose breakpoints by content fit, not device marketing names.
- Keep article text in a readable measure, approximately 60–75 characters on wider screens; comfortable mobile text size and line spacing matter more than an exact number.
- Keep visible labels and correct autocomplete. Preserve first name, last name and email in the subscription journey.
- Aim for approximately 44-pixel controls for comfortable touch. WCAG 2.2 AA’s target-size criterion specifies 24×24 CSS pixels with stated exceptions/spacing alternatives; do not mislabel 44 pixels as its universal AA minimum.
- Ensure focus is visible and not hidden behind sticky navigation. Support reduced motion and avoid hover-only information.
- Do not put horizontal carousels between readers and essential information. A swipe-only interaction is not a substitute for navigation.
- Provide meaningful photo alternatives and captions where context matters; describe charts in text and tables.
- Preserve the private/public distinction on phones: Writer, tokens, inboxes and evidence attachments must never enter public analytics or indexing.

Target good field Core Web Vitals at the 75th percentile: **LCP ≤2.5 seconds, INP ≤200 ms, CLS ≤0.1**. Validate on actual devices and networks when data is available. CI, a desktop screenshot and a single lab score do not certify field performance.

## 9. Global SEO, discovery and responsible AI

The global English foundation is already implemented. The next gains should come from useful original content, clear entities, context and internal links.

- Use clear titles that identify the problem and context; explain local terms for international readers without removing Kenyan/African specificity.
- Maintain the canonical .com identity and path-preserving aliases. Keep public-only sitemap and accurate modification dates.
- Match structured data to visible content. Person/ProfilePage/Article/WebPage identity should remain consistent; add video or resource-specific data only when the underlying content supports it.
- Use genuine language versions with translation review before adding hreflang. Do not create near-duplicate pages for every country and territory or redirect readers by IP.
- Explain authorship, dates, sources and uncertainty in the page itself. Structured data cannot rescue an unsupported impact claim.
- Google’s AI-search guidance does not require a separate AI schema or a guaranteed-placement technique. Keep content crawlable, textual, well-linked and useful; do not promise inclusion in AI Overviews or AI Mode.
- Use AI inside the existing reviewed drafting workflow for outlines, structure, clarity and metadata suggestions. Do not auto-publish unverified facts or process private messages/attachments without the appropriate selection and consent.
- A source ledger should keep the URL, access date, evidence, applicability and author review for a substantive claim. No benchmark’s article should be republished as your own.
- Connect native GSC and GA4 reporting when the Google credentials are ready. Measure discovery and meaningful reader actions; do not purchase another SEO subscription merely for this report.

## 10. Subscription, social and private operations

The new email design already provides the appropriate foundation. Retain confirmed opt-in, clear preferences, immediate one-click opt-out, distinct transactional messages and the six-profile footer. Finish Workspace delivery and test real inbox rendering before calling the email system operational.

On public pages, keep website content the durable source. Link to approved personal social profiles with a short description of their purpose where useful. Avoid loading six live social feeds or tracking embeds on every page.

Inside Writer, organise by task: capture, draft, review, approve, publish, reconcile and learn. Show each platform’s actual connection, supported formats and last checked state. Do not present a saved app secret as a connected account or an upload acceptance as a published post.

Keep Facebook professional-profile posting assisted unless an actual supported route exists. Separate member publishing from organisation/Page publishing on other platforms. Do not change an account into an organisation account to satisfy an API.

### Measures that would make the work assessable

Use a small task-based usability exercise with new readers, entrepreneurs and potential collaborators. Ask them to find a starting article, explain your role in a project, locate the source of a goal figure, use a resource and unsubscribe without assistance. Record task completion and confusion; this is a proposed test, not measured evidence.

For live reporting, distinguish collection from reporting permission. Track successful searches, useful downloads, episode continuation, confirmed subscriptions, relevant enquiries and delivered/published outcomes. Do not treat raw views, follower totals or a click on an outbound link as proof of social impact.

## 12. Avoid these tempting changes

Do not copy institutional mega-menus; introduce public account walls for ordinary writing; fill empty grids with invented stories; display unsupported media logos; copy competitors’ testimonials; manufacture beneficiary totals; add bulk country doorway pages; publish machine translations without review; autoplay large background video; scatter chat popups across mobile reading; turn the private Media Centre into a public page; expose private MCP tools; or promise all social features without platform eligibility.

The intended design is **easier to understand, better evidenced and easier to maintain**, with the owner’s voice and African perspective intact.
