I come from a village in Endarasha, Nyeri County, at the foot of the Nyandarua Mountains, looking towards Mount Kenya. The distance between that beginning and the work I do today has not been a straight line. It has been shaped by people who opened doors, difficult choices, business setbacks, faith, and a determination to make the opportunities I have received count for something beyond myself.

I believe a meaningful life is inseparable from the well-being of other people and the natural world around us.

This is the first story I want to share on my renewed website. Not a list of titles or achievements, but an introduction to the experiences, convictions and questions behind my work—and an invitation to explore them together.

Where my story begins

I was born in Nyeri Town and grew up around Charity and Gakanga in Endarasha, rural Nyeri County, Kenya. The landscape shaped my earliest understanding of life: farms and forests, roads and water, families working hard, and communities in which opportunity could never be taken for granted.

I have known difficult circumstances from childhood, and I know something of what poverty means. Those experiences taught me that where a person begins should not determine the limits of their future. They also taught me something equally important: determination matters, but so do access, encouragement and the people willing to help others move forward.

I do not believe progress comes from waiting for circumstances to become perfect. I believe in taking responsibility for the next useful step, using whatever resources are available, and remaining open to help, learning and collaboration along the way.

The people who opened doors

My educational journey was not straightforward. I encountered obstacles, changed environments, and relied on people who saw possibilities in me. Among them were the Salesians of Don Bosco, whose support made it possible for me to complete my high school education. I later pursued studies in International Relations and Diplomacy, alongside further professional learning.

I remain grateful to the people and institutions who gave their time, resources and encouragement when they were most needed. Their support shaped a conviction I carry into my work today: an opportunity offered to one person can ripple outward to many others.

Faith is also an important part of my life. I give thanks to God for life, health and the strength to keep going. Gratitude, for me, is not only something to express. It is something to act upon—by creating opportunities, sharing knowledge and helping where I can.

What building—and failing—has taught me

My journey in enterprise has brought both progress and difficult lessons. I have started and worked with businesses, made decisions I would approach differently today, and experienced the pressure of late salaries, unpaid customer invoices, supplier obligations and challenging financing arrangements.

I do not tell those stories to romanticise hardship or present every setback as a success in disguise. When a business struggles, real people carry the consequences. Leadership demands accountability, better systems and the willingness to correct course.

Those experiences explain why I care about careful planning, sound business models, responsible growth and results that can be demonstrated—not merely promised. They also remind me that learning is rarely finished. I continue to build, question assumptions, test ideas and begin again when necessary.

My idea of happiness begins with harmony

When I ask myself what I ultimately want from life, the answer is simple: to be happy. Yet I cannot separate personal happiness from what is happening around me. If people are living without dignity or opportunity, or if the environment that sustains us is being damaged, something essential is out of balance.

That is why I care about the relationship between people and nature. Communities need livelihoods, education and infrastructure. They also need healthy land, water, ecosystems and a future that does not come at the expense of the next generation.

For me, a fulfilling life means contributing, in whatever measure I can, to a world where people and nature can thrive together.

Why social entrepreneurship matters to me

I am particularly drawn to social entrepreneurship: building organisations and enterprises that address meaningful social or environmental problems while developing models capable of sustaining their work.

I have learned that lasting change rarely happens because one individual or institution acts alone. Communities understand their priorities. Entrepreneurs test practical solutions. Schools, researchers, businesses, investors, public institutions and civil society each bring capabilities that the others may lack. Working together is not always easy, but it is often where the most useful possibilities emerge.

My interests extend across agribusiness, infrastructure, technology, innovation, education, market access and enterprise development. The connecting question is the same: how can an idea become a practical solution that improves lives, respects nature and stands up to honest measurement?

The work I do

Much of my professional life centres on developing strategies, building ventures, solving operational problems and bringing people together around shared goals. I am interested in business development, digital strategy, marketing, partnerships and the practical work of starting or growing enterprises in Kenya and beyond.

I value clear objectives, disciplined implementation and measurable outcomes. But I also know that a spreadsheet, a strategy or a well-designed website is only useful when it connects with the needs of real people.

If you are exploring a business idea, a collaboration or a social initiative, I welcome thoughtful conversations about what you are trying to achieve and where our experiences might be useful to each other.

Why I am writing here

This website is my personal space for self-expression, learning and connection. It is where I will share My Thoughts—not as final answers, but as views formed through experience and open to better evidence.

You will find reflections on social entrepreneurship, agribusiness, infrastructure, innovation and travel, alongside stories about people, places, business decisions, disappointments, discoveries and the lessons that stay with me.

I want to write with honesty rather than perform perfection. I hope these stories help someone ask a better question, reconsider an assumption, try a useful next step or feel encouraged to share their own experience. I also hope to learn from readers whose backgrounds and perspectives differ from mine.

An invitation to connect

If you have reached this far, thank you for taking the time to read the beginning of my story.

This is not a journey I expect everyone to walk in the same way. But I believe we can learn from one another—and that our experiences, ideas and connections can create impact far beyond ourselves.

Welcome. I am glad you are here.
